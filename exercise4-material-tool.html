Manufacturing Process of Aluminum Alloy Windows

I. Three Main Material Categories for Window Components

Metal Materials (Aluminum Alloy Profiles): Window frames, sashes, mullions, and pressure lines (edge strips for fixing glass). Typically uses 6063-T5 aluminum alloy, known for high strength, corrosion resistance, and lightweight properties.

Polymer Materials (PVC/PA66/EPDM Rubber):

  • PA66 GF25 (Nylon + Glass Fiber): Thermal break strips used in thermal break aluminum structures to block heat transfer.
  • PVC/ABS: Corner keys, rollers, and outer casings for window handles.
  • EPDM Rubber (Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer): Sealing strips for sealing between glass and profiles, and between sashes and frames.

Inorganic Non-metallic Materials (Float Glass/Tempered Glass): Glass panels (typically double or triple insulating tempered glass) that provide lighting, sound insulation, and thermal insulation.

II. Metal Component Production Process (Aluminum Alloy Profile Extrusion and Processing)

1. Raw Material Preparation

Aluminum Billets: Use 6063 aluminum alloy round ingots that have undergone homogenization treatment to eliminate internal stresses.

2. Complete Processing Flow

Hot Extrusion Molding
Aluminum billets are heated to 480°C-520°C and pushed through an extrusion machine into specific cross-sectional shape dies, producing extruded aluminum profiles (such as frame profiles, sash profiles).

Air Cooling Quenching and Aging Treatment
Immediately after extrusion, the profiles undergo air cooling quenching, followed by aging in an oven at around 200°C to achieve T5 temper (Webster hardness ≥8-12).

Surface Treatment (Spraying/Electrophoresis/Wood Grain Transfer Printing)

  • Pre-treatment: Alkali etching and acid cleaning to remove surface oil and oxide layers.
  • Spraying: Electrostatic powder coating or fluorocarbon spraying to form a 60-80μm protective layer with excellent weather resistance.
  • Wood Grain Transfer: Apply wood grain patterns to powder-coated surfaces using heat transfer technology for a wood-like appearance.

Thermal Break Insertion (Thermal Break Process)
For thermal break aluminum windows, PA66 thermal break strips are inserted into profile grooves using an insertion machine, and then mechanically bonded through rolling compaction, creating a "thermal break" structure that combines inner and outer aluminum profiles with the thermal break strip.

Precision Cutting and CNC Machining
Use double-head precision saws to cut at 45° or 90° angles, and CNC machining centers to mill drainage holes, hardware installation slots, and lock holes.

III. Auxiliary Component Manufacturing Processes (Thermal Break Strips and Sealing Strips)

1. Thermal Break Strip (PA66 GF25) Extrusion Process

  • Material Drying: PA66 nylon pellets need to be dried at 120°C for over 4 hours.
  • Extrusion Molding: Molten plastic is extruded through a die to form "I" or "T" shapes.
  • Drawing and Shaping: Pass through a cooling water bath for shaping, with added glass fibers for enhanced strength.

2. Sealing Strip (EPDM) Extrusion and Vulcanization

  • Mixing and Extrusion: Rubber raw materials are mixed and then extruded through an extruder to form strip shapes.
  • High Temperature Vulcanization: Use microwave vulcanization chambers or salt bath vulcanization to cross-link rubber molecules, providing high elasticity and aging resistance.

IV. Glass Component Manufacturing Process (Insulating Tempered Glass)

1. Material Composition

  • Base Glass: Float glass
  • Spacer Layer: Aluminum spacer bars (filled with molecular sieve desiccant)
  • Sealing: Butyl rubber (first seal) + polysulfide rubber/silicone rubber (second seal)

2. Production Process

Cutting and Edge Grinding
Automatic cutting machines cut glass to size, followed by precision edge grinding to prevent stress concentration that could lead to breakage.

  • Physical Tempering
    Glass is heated to 650°C-700°C and then rapidly cooled in a blast furnace. This process creates compressive stress on the glass surface, increasing strength by 3-5 times. When broken, it shatters into small particles, making it safer.
  • Insulating Glass Assembly
    ① Cleaning and drying: Remove dust and moisture from glass surfaces;
    ② Lamination: Place aluminum spacer bars coated with butyl rubber between two glass panels;
    ③ Sealing: Use a two-component dispensing machine to apply the second sealant, creating a sealed, dry gas layer (air or argon) for sound and thermal insulation.

V. Window Assembly Process

1. Window Frame/Sash Assembly

  • Corner Joining: Apply corner glue to 45° cuts on profiles, then mechanically join using corner keys and a corner joining machine to ensure seamless, waterproof, and sturdy connections.
  • Hardware Installation: Install transmission boxes, locking points, hinges, and window handles.
